# Naturalness improvement plan for Sinhala TTS

## Decision

### New-model route

When the original target speakers are unavailable, train a separate Sinhala
voice from the available corpus rather than adapting Roshan or Nipunika. The
current implementation derives six acoustic style controls—calm, intimate,
conversational, lively, energetic, and expressive—from pitch, energy, speaking
rate, and silence. These are controllable delivery styles, not emotion labels.

The implementation is in `scripts/derive_prosody_styles.py` and
`scripts/train_vits_natural_style_gpu.py`. It uses the original checkpoint
only for architecture/configuration; it does not restore its weights.

Sinhala podcast speech is a promising **style and prosody source**, but it should
not be mixed directly into the current VITS training set. The best route for this
project is a controlled two-part improvement:

1. keep a clean, single-speaker corpus as the pronunciation and voice anchor;
2. add rights-cleared conversational and emotional speech through a separate
   speaker/style-conditioned adaptation experiment.

This separates three goals that are often confused:

| Goal | Data that teaches it | Recommended control |
|---|---|---|
| Sinhala pronunciation and text coverage | clean read speech with exact transcripts | Sinhala normalization + grapheme/phonology frontend |
| conversational naturalness | relaxed interviews, podcasts, dialogue, turn-taking | reference-prompt or style conditioning |
| sad/empathetic expression | explicitly labelled sad/empathetic performances from the target speaker | emotion/style reference or style token |

Generic podcasts can improve rhythm, pauses, pitch variation, and reduced-form
conversation. They do **not** automatically teach sadness. A podcast may be
informative, humorous, formal, excited, or emotionally neutral even when the
speaker sounds natural.

## Evaluation of the proposed podcast/subtitle method

### Why it can help

- Podcast speech contains phrase-final intonation, natural pause placement,
  variable speaking rate, emphasis, repairs, and discourse markers that are
  underrepresented in read-speech corpora.
- Subtitles or transcripts provide a starting text/audio pairing and make
  long-form material much easier to search and segment.
- A single consenting host can provide a coherent target voice and style. This
  is much safer for a single-speaker adaptation than mixing unrelated actors.

### Main failure modes

1. **Rights and voice consent.** Public availability is not permission to train
   a voice model or redistribute generated speech. Keep a written permission
   record for each source and speaker. The permission must cover downloading or
   receiving the source, ML training, derived checkpoints, demo output, and
   publication.
2. **Subtitle mismatch.** Captions can omit fillers, paraphrase speech, merge
   turns, contain timing drift, or be generated by ASR. TTS requires the text to
   match what was actually spoken. A subtitle file is not ground-truth by
   itself.
3. **Contamination.** Podcast audio often includes music, jingles, ads, room
   reverberation, laughter, crosstalk, telephone audio, clipping, and several
   speakers. These can become audible artifacts in the synthesized voice.
4. **Speaker leakage and identity blending.** If several hosts and guests are
   mixed into one speaker model, the model may become unstable or acquire an
   unintended voice. Preserve speaker IDs and either train one target speaker
   or use a genuinely multi-speaker model.
5. **Style overfitting.** A model fine-tuned only on emotional or highly
   animated material may sound unnatural on ordinary text, overuse dramatic
   pitch, or lose pronunciation learned from the clean corpus.
6. **Selection bias.** Keeping only the most expressive clips can make a demo
   sound emotional while reducing robustness. Keep neutral conversational clips
   as the majority and evaluate on unseen neutral, conversational, and emotional
   text separately.

### Verdict

The method is worthwhile as a **licensed conversational adaptation dataset**,
not as an unfiltered podcast scrape. Its expected benefit is high for
conversational naturalness, medium for general intelligibility, and low for
sadness unless the clips have explicit emotion labels or a reference prompt
captures that emotion.

## Recommended data hierarchy

Use three data tiers and do not silently combine them:

### Tier A — gold TTS data

Clean mono audio, one audible speaker, manually corrected transcript, natural
utterance boundaries, and verified permission. This is the only tier used for
the main voice-quality experiment.

### Tier B — filtered conversational data

Rights-cleared podcast/interview speech with manual transcript correction,
speaker labels, quality measurements, and style labels. Use it for a
conversational/style adaptation, with lower sampling weight than Tier A at the
start.

### Tier C — ASR or prosody resources

Noisy, multi-speaker, weakly labelled, or redistribution-restricted speech.
Use it for Sinhala ASR, forced-alignment development, speaker diarization, or
prosody analysis—not directly for the first TTS acoustic model.

The existing PathNirvana male subset remains the Tier A anchor. The candidate
caption-derived Sinhala TTS release and the SiTa in-the-wild corpus should be
audited into Tier B or Tier C only after their source rights and transcript
quality are independently confirmed.

## Stronger method than “train on podcasts”

### 1. Build a target-speaker conversational set

The highest-value experiment is 5–10 hours recorded or supplied by one
consenting Sinhala speaker who will be used as the target voice. Record the same
speaker in multiple modes:

- 50% neutral and conversational explanations;
- 15% questions, answers, requests, and short dialogue turns;
- 15% empathetic/sad delivery;
- 10% emphasis, surprise, and encouragement;
- 10% numerals, dates, names, code-switching, and pronunciation challenge text.

Record multiple versions of a smaller set of sentences. This gives the model
prosodic variation without mixing identities and provides paired examples for a
style-control demo.

### 2. Use authorized podcasts as supplementary data

Prefer original audio and scripts supplied by the creator. If only subtitles
are available, use them as alignment hypotheses, then manually correct every
retained utterance. Do not make a scraper the project pipeline.

Required automatic filters are duration, speech probability, clipping, SNR,
transcript/audio CER, repetition rate, and speaker consistency. Manually listen
to every clip used in the final small experiment and at least a random audit of
the larger set.

### 3. Condition style instead of baking it into one voice

For the proposed F5/SiFi route, use a 4–10 second reference prompt from the same
speaker and label the prompt style (`neutral`, `conversational`, `sad`,
`empathetic`, etc.). At inference, select a reference prompt that expresses the
desired style. A compact style token can be added as an ablation, but the
reference-prompt path should be implemented first because it is simpler and
more faithful to the available supervision.

For the current VITS baseline, keep a neutral model and a conversational
adapted checkpoint separate. Do not overwrite the baseline checkpoint and do
not use mixed-speaker podcast audio for the single-speaker VITS model.

### 4. Preserve pronunciation with mixture sampling

Start the adaptation with approximately:

```text
60% clean Tier A read speech
30% Tier B conversational speech
10% explicitly emotional speech
```

Then compare 0%, 20%, and 30% conversational sampling at equal optimizer steps.
If the emotional set is small, oversample it only after checking for memorized
prosody and keep a speaker/source-disjoint evaluation set. Rehearsal batches
from Tier A reduce catastrophic forgetting.

## Minimal experimental matrix

Use the same held-out text, speaker prompt, random seeds, and hardware for all
conditions:

| ID | Training data | Style input | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| B0 | existing Dialog/UoM VITS | none | external baseline |
| B1 | Tier A only | neutral prompt | clean adaptation baseline |
| C1 | Tier A + conversational Tier B | neutral prompt | tests conversational data |
| C2 | Tier A + conversational Tier B | conversational prompt | tests style transfer |
| E1 | Tier A + conversational + emotional data | sad/empathetic prompt | tests emotional naturalness |
| S1 | C2 + Sinhala feature frontend/PEFT | conversational prompt | proposed SiFi-TTS system |

The important comparison is C1 versus B1 at matched steps and speaker data.
Otherwise an improvement cannot be attributed to conversational data.

## Evaluation that measures “natural” rather than only “correct”

Automatic ASR CER/WER is necessary but cannot measure naturalness. Use a
blinded native-speaker study with the same text across systems and collect at
least:

- naturalness MOS (1–5);
- intelligibility (1–5 or transcription accuracy);
- conversationalness preference;
- sad/empathetic appropriateness for emotional items;
- speaker similarity and obvious artifact flags.

Report results separately for neutral, conversational, and emotional subsets.
Include a natural human recording as an upper reference and keep all random
seeds rather than selecting the best sample. Use paired confidence intervals or
paired bootstrap tests because every listener should hear matched system/text
pairs.

Recommended success criteria for the first iteration:

- conversational MOS improves over B1 without a meaningful neutral-MOS drop;
- emotional appropriateness improves over C1 on the emotional subset;
- Sinhala CER does not regress by more than 5% relative on the pronunciation
  challenge set;
- speaker identity remains stable across styles;
- the system remains near real time for normal sentence lengths.

## Practical order of work

1. Implement/verify text normalization and keep the current baseline unchanged.
2. Create a rights manifest and collect 1–2 hours from one target speaker as a
   smoke-test subset.
3. Manually verify 500–1,000 utterances and run the data quality report.
4. Fine-tune B1, C1, and C2 for a short equal-step pilot.
5. Listen to matched samples before spending on a full run.
6. Add the emotional subset and E1 only after conversational adaptation is
   stable.
7. Run the blinded evaluation and select the best data/style recipe, not just
   the most dramatic sample.

The new `scripts/prepare_conversational_data.py` utility enforces the license,
transcript, duration, speaker, and source checks before producing a training
manifest. By default it also requires permission to release the trained model;
use `--allow-private-only` only for an experiment that will not publish a
checkpoint or derived demo. It intentionally does not download media or claim
that captions are ground truth.
